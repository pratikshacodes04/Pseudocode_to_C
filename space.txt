Telemetry is the stream of data that satellites send about their health, position and operations. When this data is compromised, scientists may misinterpret the satellite's condition, leading to invalid commands or loss of the satellite. Since satellites support services like GPS navigation, internet connectivity, weather forecasting, disaster monitoring and defence systems, any disruption in telemetry can affect aviation, communication, disaster responses and national security. Moreover, when anomalies occur, scientists are unable to distinguish between natural faults or cyber-attacks, which might delay or make wrong decisions, leading to satellite damage and infrastructure failures. In countries such as India, increasingly depend on satellite-based systems to support agriculture monitoring, transportation networks and digital payments. Since these compromised satellite could disrupt daily life and national security, Hence making telemetry protection important for both global and local stability.

The proposed framework adopts a sequential dual-layer defense approach designed to distinguish routine mechanical faults from advanced cyber-attacks in orbital telemetry. Satellite telemetry data containing system health, position, and logs are collected and divided into time-based segments using a sliding-window method. NASA SMAP and MSL datasets are used as benchmark datasets. Pre-processing normalizes features with MinMaxScaler to keep inputs consistent and performs data cleaning to eliminate noise, producing stable inputs. The  LSTM-Autoencoder identifies anomalies based on reconstruction-loss, while  XGBoost engine performs cross-checks the telemetry against verified system logs. Events are classified using the SPARTA-indexed IOB framework, by linking telemetry patterns to operational states, enabling accurate separation between real faults and spoofed  data. Real-time alerts and automated mitigation responses are delivered to satellite operators through secure dashboard, reducing false positives and operator uncertainty. The dual-engine model strengthens cybersecurity, protects high-value satellite assets, and maintains reliable infrastructure services worldwide.

The total estimated budget for the proposed research framework is 5,00,000 INR, distributed across six major domains. The largest portion of the funding, 2,00,000 INR is allocated to Personnel and Human Resources to support stipends for developers, data analysts, and research assistants assigned with model architecture and system integration. Computing Infrastructure requires 90,000 INR to secure high-performance GPU resources for training the LSTM-Autoencoder, while 80,000 INR supports data management, including telemetry storage and dataset preparation.The remaining capital is divided between Software Development Tools (50,000 INR) for cloud credits and specialized libraries, and Hardware Integration (50,000 INR) including workstations and networking components required for the system prototype. Contingency fund of 30,000INR is reserved for testing protocols, documentation, and unforeseen technical requirements during the deployment phase. This balanced allocation ensures that both the computational demands of the dual-engine model and the operational integrity of the satellite telemetry environment are adequately maintained.
